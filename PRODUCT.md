# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS: a single hand-written `index.html`, no build step, no framework. Keep it that way unless the owner changes it.

## Users

Primary: people who have come across one of Logic and Matter's products and want to know who makes it. They arrive with little context, look around briefly, and then leave for a product, its docs, or email. Potential consulting clients and interested peers also visit, but they come second.

## Product Purpose

logicandmatter.com is the home page for Logic and Matter, a one-person studio that writes software, ships products, and offers consulting. Its job is to be a clear hub: it tells visitors who is behind the products and sends them on to the right product or service. It works if a visitor quickly finds the thing they came for and trusts that a real person stands behind it.

## Positioning

A one-person studio. The same person builds the products and does the consulting. Do not present it as a company with a team.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- One page listing Products and Services (consulting), plus a footer with a contact email.
- Services is currently consulting only: "Architecture, prototyping, and product engineering." Clients reach it by email.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: "Logic and Matter". Legal entity: Logic and Matter, LLC (used in the copyright line). The ampersand is the studio mark.
- Contact: hello@logicandmatter.com.
- Voice (from the existing copy): plain, short, and understated. No marketing puffery.

## Evidence on Hand

Products (real, linked from the home page): variart (variart.io), seek (seek.io), overplayed (overplayed.app), ScoutDays (scoutdays.app), threesixfive (threesixfive.photos). The one-liners describe what each product does, based on its own site. threesixfive makes printed calendars with one of your photos every day (description from the owner). There are no testimonials, clients or metrics; don't fabricate any.

## Product Principles

1. Get people to the product they came for. The site is a hub, not the destination.
2. Be honest about scale: one person, a small number of finished products.
3. Keep it light: one static page that loads instantly and is easy to maintain by hand.
4. Present the work as finished products. Never label anything as an experiment, side project or work in progress.
5. Understate the copy. Say plainly what something does, in one line.
